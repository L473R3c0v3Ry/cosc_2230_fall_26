3. Why fragment 5 is still quadratic

Its count is n(n - 1)/2 = (1/2)n^2 - (1/2)n. The leading term is proportional to n^2. Multiplying by a constant such as 1/2 does not change the order of growth, and the linear term becomes less significant as n increases. Therefore, fragment 5 is Theta(n^2), just like fragment 3.

4. Fragment 6: prediction and comparison

The analytical prediction is Theta(n), linear growth. The outer loop uses i = 1, 2, 4, 8, ... while i < n, and the inner loop runs i times. Thus the total is a geometric sum, not n times the number of outer iterations.

For n > 1, let m = ceil(log2(n)). The exact count is:
1 + 2 + 4 + ... + 2^(m - 1) = 2^m - 1.
This count is at least n - 1 and less than 2n, so its growth is linear.

At n = 74, the count is 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 + 64 = 127.
At n = 148, it is 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 + 64 + 128 = 255.
The ratio is 255/127, about 2.00787. These results support the linear prediction. Having nested loops does not automatically mean quadratic growth because their iteration counts matter.
